Where to bring it in.

Two records can share a tempo and a key and still wreck the mix — the kicks land on different sixteenths, or both singers arrive at once. Cuepoint scores your crate on seven things instead of two, then plays the transition back so you can check it by ear.

│ mix point │ bass swap · drag it │ playhead

Both records on one axis, lined up at the bar where one becomes the other. Colours are the phrase — intro, build, peak, breakdown, outro.

Beginner Plain English, a difficulty label on every blend, and the bars counted out along the grid.
Expert The seven scoring components as measured. Switch any time in the header.
1

Pick the record that's playing. The list on the left. Search it, or filter to what's actually mixable — pitch range, Camelot key and genre.

2

Read the score. Every track rated 0–1. Beginner mode labels each one clean, needs EQ, tricky or advanced — start with the clean ones.

3

Take the mix point. Where to leave the outgoing record, where to come in on the new one, how many bars to run together, and the pitch fader move.

4

Hear it before you play it. Press ▶ on any result. The outgoing runs for eight bars, the incoming enters at its cue pitched to match, and they crossfade over exactly the bars chosen.

5

Shape it while it runs. Drag the lavender line to move where the bass changes hands. Cut lows, mids or highs on either deck, sweep the filter, or throw an echo timed to the bar. If a vocal is still going on the way out, it's held under the new record to finish rather than being cut.

6

Fix what clashes. Where a blend is weak the card names it — two kicks beating, two vocals at once, kicks on different sixteenths — and says which band to pull.
